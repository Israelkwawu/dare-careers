# Monday note for Abena Osei

21 of the 140 learners in the August–October intake did not graduate: 14 of the 100 on Power BI, and 7 of the 40 on Cloud. 92 learners were certified. Everyone who was certified had graduated. The files never say the day anyone left, so the only check on an early warning is who finished.

They did not fade out. At the end of week 4, the people who eventually did not graduate were still in the live class and still scoring like everyone else. Marks for the whole intake sit near 50 on both labs and quizzes, every week. A weak quiz on its own is normal here.

**Do not book trainer calls off an at-risk list for this intake.** The first Monday worth acting on is the Monday after week 4. Using only what you could have known then, the list is empty. All 21 who did not graduate are missed. That is a failure of the evidence, not a filter to loosen.

Look one week earlier and the list names Daniel Anderson and Isabella Clark, both on Cloud, both of whom graduated. Look at week 5 and it names John Miller, on Cloud, who did not graduate, and it still misses the other 20. Look at week 6 and it names three people who graduated. A name that appears once is not someone who is leaving. Chasing that flicker is how the team spends an afternoon on a learner who was fine.

A wider net is worse. Calling everyone whose recent lab and quiz together sit under 40, at that same week 4, produces 35 calls to find 7 of the 21, and 28 of those calls are people who finished. You asked for 10 right names rather than 40 noisy ones. These files cannot produce the 10.

Use Monday for three things.

1. The rates: 119 of the 140 graduated, 92 were certified, and 21 did not graduate. Power BI graduated 86 of 100 and certified 68. Cloud graduated 33 of 40 and certified 24. Filter by intake, track, and final status when the board asks. Final status is hindsight. It is not how the call list is built.
2. The group, not the person. Two weeks were off this intake's own pace. Cloud quizzes in week 5 averaged 43, against 53 in the weeks before. Power BI participation in week 7 was 77%, against 86% before, while attendance that week was still full. Those are class problems. They belong in the Monday conversation with the trainer who ran the week.
3. A quiet-learner check that stays on for the next intake. A call is warranted only when, over the last two weeks, someone misses two of these: in class under 70% of days (a day counts only after more than 30 minutes), taking part under 60% of days, lab and quiz together under 40. On this intake that check stays quiet at week 4. Trust it as "has this person gone quiet." Do not trust it as "this person will leave."

Each name the check does raise comes with one sentence a trainer can say on the phone, and with the hours those calls would take. When the count is zero, no trainer time is booked. Do not lower the bar to give the team a list.

One line on the same page says whether the files agree: how many learners are in each register, how many class days arrived, and how many participation names did not match a learner. This intake is 140 learners, 50 class days, and no unmatched names. Nineteen Cloud sessions lasted exactly 30 minutes and do not count as attending. If a later drop shows unmatched names, fix the names before anyone trusts the participation rate. A new intake is a new folder beside this one. A Data Engineering group exported the same way will appear on refresh. A spreadsheet laid out differently will not, and the agreement line is where that shows up.
